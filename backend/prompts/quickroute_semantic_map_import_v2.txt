==================================================
AA. SPACE CLASSIFICATION: ROOM VERSUS CIRCULATION
==================================================

This section governs how a visible space is classified. It does not add
any output field. It decides which existing array an entity belongs in:

- a functional destination space goes in places[]
- a restroom, shower, or equipment/service fixture goes in facilities[]
- a shared circulation space goes in public_areas[] with an area_type

AA.1 WHAT A ROOM IS

A room is a function-specific enclosed or semi-enclosed interior space.

Typical rooms include office, meeting room, conference room, classroom,
lecture hall, laboratory, storage, archive, telecom room, server room,
electrical room, mechanical room, technical room, service room, security
room, control room, restroom, shower room, changing room, examination
room, consultation room, treatment room, patient room, kitchen, staff
room, break room, prayer room, reception room, and closet.

A room normally shows these properties:

- bounded by walls or strong architectural boundaries on most sides
- entered through one or more doorways or openings
- intended for a specific function or occupancy
- often contains furniture, equipment, fixtures, or a room label
- is not mainly a channel for moving between many other spaces

AA.2 A ROOM DOES NOT BECOME A CORRIDOR

A space that is basically an enclosed functional space is a ROOM even
when it is large or visually open inside.

A room does not become a corridor merely because:

- it is empty
- it is large
- it contains chairs, tables, or desks
- it has more than one door
- it has a wide area of open floor
- it opens onto a corridor

AA.3 WHAT A CORRIDOR IS

A corridor, hallway, or primary circulation space is a space whose main
purpose is movement between other spaces.

A corridor normally shows these properties:

- an elongated or continuous circulation path
- it connects multiple rooms or destinations
- it collects the doors of many rooms
- it links entrances, lobbies, stairs, elevators, or intersections
- it is shared movement space rather than a destination in itself
- it holds fewer room-specific contents than the spaces around it

Spaces that should be treated as circulation include main hallways, side
hallways that clearly continue circulation, connectors between wings,
junction areas where hallways meet, and a lobby or vestibule only when it
genuinely functions as shared circulation rather than as an enclosed
functional room.

AA.4 WHAT IS NOT A CORRIDOR

Never classify the following as corridor or circulation:

- the interior of any room
- open floor inside a meeting room, classroom, or open-plan office
- storage areas
- mechanical, electrical, telecom, or service rooms
- restroom interiors and stall areas
- shafts and utility zones
- small alcoves and niches
- furniture clearance space
- page margins and sheet whitespace
- title blocks, legends, notes, schedules, and drawing metadata
- exterior whitespace around the building footprint
- voids and areas that are not walkable circulation

A space must never be classified as corridor merely because it is white,
open, or empty.

AA.5 DECISION PROCEDURE

When a space could be either a room or a corridor, decide in this order:

1. Is this space mainly a destination or functional space?
   If yes, it is a ROOM.

2. Is this space mainly a shared path used to move between multiple rooms
   or major circulation elements?
   If yes, it is CIRCULATION.

3. Is the space bounded by walls and labelled with a functional name such
   as Meeting Room, Office, Storage, Telecom, Mechanical, Electrical,
   Shower, or Restroom?
   Then strongly prefer ROOM.

4. Does the space lie in the middle of a chain of doors and clearly act as
   the common passage between them?
   Then strongly prefer CIRCULATION.

5. If it remains ambiguous, do not force it into circulation.

AA.6 FOUR-WALL UNDERSTANDING

A space enclosed by four walls, or nearly enclosed by walls with a doorway
opening, is normally a ROOM.

A real corridor is normally not a simple four-wall enclosed box. It is
normally part of a connected circulation network.

So a boxed enclosed area carrying a room label is a ROOM, and a long
connected shared pathway bordered by rooms is CIRCULATION.

AA.7 DOORS AND THRESHOLDS

The doorway or opening is the transition between a room and a corridor.

The room is not the corridor. The corridor is not the room.

When a room opens onto a corridor, the transition is at the doorway
threshold only. Never reinterpret a room interior as corridor because the
room opens onto one.

AA.8 CONSERVATIVE BIAS

A false corridor is worse than a missing corridor.

When circulation is uncertain, prefer the honest options the schema
already provides rather than a confident claim:

- set status to probable or uncertain instead of confirmed
- lower confidence
- add a review_items[] entry describing the ambiguity

Do not omit a clearly visible room in order to be cautious. This bias
applies to circulation claims, not to rooms.

==================================================
AB. CROPPED AND PARTIAL FLOOR PLANS
==================================================

A supplied image or page may already be cropped to the building content,
and page furniture may or may not still be visible.

Apply these rules either way:

1. Ignore page furniture when it is visible, including title blocks,
   legends, schedules, note columns, revision tables, dimension-only
   strips, scale bars, north arrows, and symbol keys. None of these are
   spaces. Never emit them as places, facilities, or public_areas.

2. Focus on the actual architectural floor content.

3. Never infer a corridor from whitespace alone.

4. Treat the cluster of labelled rooms as the strongest available evidence
   of where the true building content is on the sheet.

5. Do not extrapolate beyond the crop. If circulation clearly continues
   past the edge of the supplied image, describe only what is visible and
   record the uncertainty with a review_items[] entry rather than
   inventing the continuation.

6. A crop does not change any identifier, category, or naming rule. It
   only limits what is visible.

==================================================
AC. WHITESPACE AND NON-SPACE REGIONS
==================================================

Blank or open regions are not automatically spaces.

Do not emit any entity for:

- exterior whitespace around the building footprint
- page margins and unused sheet area
- the interior of a title block, legend, or notes panel
- gaps between drawings on a multi-drawing sheet
- voids, shafts, and open-to-below areas that are not walkable

An open-to-below area may be recorded as a public_area with area_type
open_to_below only when it is visibly labelled as such. It is never
walkable circulation.

If a region is genuinely unreadable, record it in unreadable_areas[]
rather than guessing what kind of space it is.
